# Chapter 6 plan: Osaka (大阪) — draft for discussion

**Why Osaka:** the modern working city. Chapter 5 taught reading between the lines; chapter 6 is about the working world:
business Japanese, keigo, hierarchy, overtime, and also the warm, direct Kansai attitude. It is the bridge to Tokyo.

## Story (short)
K's note from Fushimi ended with the character 阪. In Osaka the Kotodama are **worn out**, not forgotten: phrases like
「お疲れさまです」 and 「よろしくお願いいたします」 are said thousands of times a day until they are empty. The region master
is a veteran secretary and business-manners trainer whose keigo is perfect — and who has quietly forgotten what it
feels like to say something without a formula. Maria helps a new graduate through his first weeks, listens to an
exhausted section chief, and learns that polite language is a tool for care, not only for distance.

## Society themes (each unlocks a Japan note; keep balanced)
- Overwork and overtime (残業, 過労), the "after-work drink" (飲み会) and the pressure to stay when the boss stays
- The new-graduate hiring system (新卒一括採用), first-year life, 先輩・後輩 hierarchy
- Business etiquette: business cards (名刺), bowing, 報連相 (report, inform, consult), email phrases
- Kansai vs Tokyo: more direct, humorous, food culture (たこ焼き, お好み焼き); regional identity
- Foreign workers in convenience stores and restaurants; part-time and contract work (非正規)

## People
- **Tachibana-san** (master of keigo): veteran secretary / business-manners trainer, about 58, flawless and tired
- **Kenta**: a new graduate, 23, first-year salesman, nervous and kind
- **Okan (Mrs. Naniwa)**: takoyaki stall owner, about 60, loud Kansai dialect, big heart
- **Fujita-kachō**: an overworked section chief, 45
- **Minh**: a Vietnamese part-time worker at the konbini, 24, studying Japanese for the JLPT (a mirror of Maria)
- **Station / office guide**: one more small role for a reception-desk quiz

## Learning
- Grammar focus: business and formal patterns (the remaining ~29 N2 patterns), plus keigo recap (尊敬語・謙譲語・丁寧語)
- Vocabulary: about 24 story words (残業, 名刺, 報連相, 上司, 新入社員, 取引先, 納期, 恐縮, 承知 …), more reading and listening texts
- Jobs: office front desk (reading emails / listening to phone calls), takoyaki stall (listening to orders in Kansai-ben)
