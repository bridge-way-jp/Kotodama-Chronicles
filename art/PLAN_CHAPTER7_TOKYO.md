# Chapter 7 plan: Tokyo (東京) — finale, draft for discussion

**Why Tokyo:** the biggest city, the last stop of K's trail (Kyoto 京 → Osaka 阪 → 東). Chapter 5 taught reading between the lines,
chapter 6 the working world; chapter 7 is about **living in a huge modern city** — and about Maria deciding what she does with
what she learned. Tone: lively, a little lonely, hopeful. Light touch on social issues, as in the other chapters.

## Story (short)
Tachibana-san's old notebook leads to Tokyo, where the Kotodama are **hurried and unheard**: words fly past in crowds, in
earphones and on screens and nobody listens. The region master is a night-shift radio host (深夜ラジオ) who talks to thousands of
lonely listeners and has stopped hearing his own voice. Maria and her fox follow K's last clue through four neighbourhoods, help
people who are lonely in a crowd, and finally find who K was (K's true identity and the last Kotodama of the main story: to be
confirmed by the project owner before we write it).

## Society themes (each unlocks a Japan note)
- Rush-hour trains (満員電車), manners on trains, the quiet of the commute; suica/cashless life, QR payments
- Loneliness in the crowd (孤独), single-person households, night radio, "third places" (kissaten, sento)
- Housing: tiny flats, share houses, key money, being a foreign tenant; the ward office (区役所) and paperwork (住民票, マイナンバー)
- Ageing city: old Shitamachi neighbourhoods vs. youth culture of Shibuya; festivals (祭り) that keep neighbourhoods together
- Foreign residents and tourists; English signs vs. Japanese; being addressed in English although you speak Japanese

## People
- **Rinka (radio host Mr. Kuroi)**: night-radio host, 38, warm voice, exhausted; region master
- **Haru**: share-house resident, 26, freelancer from Hokkaido, sociable on the outside, lonely at night
- **Mrs. Asakusa (Yoshie)**: kissaten owner, 72, sharp and kind
- **Yamamoto-san**: ward-office clerk, 50, patient with paperwork, a quiet hero
- **Priya**: Indian IT engineer, 30, shares a house, fluent in business Japanese, tired of being spoken to in English
- **Station attendant**: one more small role for the lost-and-found quiz
